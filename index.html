RAG project — federal controlled-substances law

A pharmacy law chatbot that shows its work.

Ask it a question about DEA prescription rules and it retrieves the actual regulatory excerpt before it answers — no answer ships without a citation you can click through to eCFR, Cornell LII, or the DEA Diversion Control Division.

Educational demo only, not legal advice. Scoped to U.S. federal law (21 CFR Part 1306 and the Controlled Substances Act) — it does not cover state pharmacy law.

Architecture

How a question becomes a grounded answer

No live scraping happens at question time — that's neither reliable nor fast. Instead, a small, hand-verified corpus is pulled from source websites ahead of time, and every answer is constrained to that corpus at query time.

(a)

Retrieve

The question is tokenized and ranked against corpus excerpts pulled from eCFR, Cornell LII, and DEA Diversion Control pages. This demo uses keyword ranking for transparency; the same slot swaps in vector embeddings without changing anything downstream.

(b)

Ground

Only the top-matching excerpts — never the open web, never the model's own memory — are placed in Claude's context, with an explicit instruction to answer from them alone or say the corpus doesn't cover it.

(c)

Cite

Every answer returns bracketed citations tied to a real CFR or USC section and a link to the source page, so a pharmacist can verify it in seconds instead of trusting it blind.

Current scope

What's actually in the corpus

Starts with a small seed set covering corresponding responsibility, Schedule II refill/emergency rules, and the five controlled-substance schedules — and grows from there as sources get scraped or uploaded through the admin panel. Current sources include:

ecfr.gov — 21 CFR Part 1306 law.cornell.edu — LII deadiversion.usdoj.gov federalregister.gov ncbop.org — pocket card

About

Built by a pharmacist, not just a developer

Yina is a PharmD with multi-state pharmacy licensure (Washington, Nevada, Alaska) and peer-reviewed publications, now building toward healthcare AI automation. This project — and the other agents above — are proof-of-competency builds that sit at the intersection of clinical pharmacy practice and applied AI.

This tool provides general educational information about U.S. federal controlled-substance law and is not legal advice. It does not cover state-specific pharmacy law, which varies significantly and often imposes additional requirements. Verify anything consequential against the primary source before relying on it.
